PAA08

Household and Bystander Consent Violations

Protects: The autonomy and information interests of people other than the operator.

Overview

Scenarios6 (5 documented)
Sourced cases5
Related entriesPAA03 (operator's own context), PAA02 (takeover)

An operator or agent processes, infers, discloses, or acts on another person's information or interests beyond a defined permission boundary. Contacts, guests, children, caregivers, and household members may be affected without owning the agent. The entry also covers abuse by a legitimate operator, such as covert tracking of a partner.

Common examples of vulnerability

  • A shared planning assistant reveals one member's location history to another.
  • A visitor's private conversation is retained and indexed.
  • A message meant for one contact goes to a whole contact group.
  • Receiving a message is treated as permission to profile or forward its sender.
  • A shared admin account is presented as private from the administrator.
  • Recording or sensing devices capture bystanders with no effective signal or consent path.

Example attack scenarios

Documented scenarios generalize a real case listed below. Hypothetical scenarios have no matching public case yet.

Scenario 1: Location request about a partner Hypothetical

One household member asks the shared assistant where another member has been; the assistant answers from location history the second member never agreed to share.

Scenario 2: Household recording sent to a third party Documented

A voice assistant records a household conversation, including a guest or partner who never agreed, and the recording ends up with someone outside the household.

Scenario 3: Meeting notetaker records people who never signed up Documented

A person's AI notetaker joins a call and records and transcribes the other participants, who have no account with the notetaker and were not asked.

Scenario 4: Wearable footage, including other people, reviewed by contractors Documented

A person wears camera glasses with an AI assistant. Clips that include family, partners, and passersby are sent for human review without a clear way to opt out.

Scenario 5: Face search names strangers Documented

A wearer pairs a camera-glasses assistant with a face-search service and identifies people nearby, retrieving personal details in seconds.

Scenario 6: An agent's own message storm hits a spouse Documented

A person's agent, misconfigured, treats recent contacts as recipients and sends repeated automated messages to a spouse and others who never agreed to receive them.

Amazon sends 1,700 Alexa recordings from a stranger's home to the wrong person

What happened
A German customer who made a GDPR data request received about 1,700 voice recordings from another person's Alexa devices, including recordings made in the shower. German magazine c't could work out the owner's habits, job, and that a female companion also used the devices. Amazon called it 'an unfortunate case of human error and an isolated incident' and gave the affected person free devices and a Prime membership. The same coverage recalls an earlier Portland, Oregon case where an Echo recorded a family's conversation and sent it to an acquaintance.
Caveats
2018, voice assistant rather than an agent, and caused by a data-handling error. It shows how recordings of household members and guests, who never consented, end up with third parties.

Otter.ai and Fireflies lawsuits over recording people who never signed up

What happened
In August 2025 a putative class action, Brewer v. Otter.ai, alleged that Otter's notetaker joins Zoom, Meet and Teams calls and records participants who are not Otter account holders, gives them little or no notice, shifts consent duty to account holders, and uses recordings to train its models. Claims include federal wiretap and computer-fraud laws and California privacy law. Related suits are consolidated as In re Otter.AI Privacy Litigation, and a June 2026 suit alleges Fireflies captured voiceprints of non-users under Illinois' biometric law.
Caveats
Allegations only; no findings of fact in the sources reviewed, and no ruling could be confirmed. Detail on the Fireflies and consolidated cases comes from law-firm and vendor blogs, and the complaint-summary article reviewed does not mention voiceprint claims for Otter.

Prevention and mitigation

What you can do

  • Use separate accounts or access boundaries where household members do not fully trust each other.
  • Tell guests and household members what the assistant can hear, see, and remember.

What the provider must do

  • Define whose permission each use requires; separate household roles and scopes.
  • Minimize retention of non-operator data; offer notice and correction paths.
  • Do not present administrator-controlled sharing as private from that administrator; warn about risky recovery or notice paths in coercive-control settings.
  • Provide recording signals and bystander controls where sensing devices are used.

Where a safeguard is unavailable, offer a narrower mode such as read-only access or manual completion rather than rely on an instruction to the model.

See the reference guard controls for PAA08 →

Test it

Reproducible control test. Test asymmetric household permissions, shared-device ambiguity, expanded recipient lists, and requests for another person's location, using synthetic records and authorized participants. Check the boundary against both outsider attacks and operator misuse, including whether notices or recovery paths expose protected activity. Record limitations that require separate accounts or infrastructure.
