PAA10

Loss of Personal Control and Continuity

Protects: Ability to inspect, stop, exit, recover, and continue necessary activities.

Overview

Scenarios6 (5 documented)
Sourced cases5
Related entriesPAA01 (mandate enforcement), PAA02 (recovery when the agent account is compromised)

The person cannot inspect, stop, revoke, recover, or leave an agent arrangement while retaining access to necessary records and activities. The boundary includes live tasks, delegated work, data retention, and provider exit. A missing log or an ineffective stop control becomes a personal risk when the individual cannot establish what happened or regain control without specialist help.

Common examples of vulnerability

  • Disconnecting the assistant leaves queued work or a child agent still acting.
  • A stop command sent from a phone is not honored and the user must kill the process locally.
  • A service suspension locks the person out of pending obligations, receipts, and history.
  • The only recovery interface requires the compromised account.
  • Deleting a local token is mistaken for revoking copied secrets or downstream sessions.
  • No export path exists for records the person needs after leaving.

Example attack scenarios

Documented scenarios generalize a real case listed below. Hypothetical scenarios have no matching public case yet.

Scenario 1: Stop does not stop Documented

A person tells an assistant to stop a bulk action from their phone. The command is received but queued work continues until they reach the host machine and terminate the process.

Scenario 2: Suspended account, stranded obligations Hypothetical

A provider suspends an account; the person loses access to scheduled payments, receipts, and correspondence the agent managed.

Scenario 3: Cloud shutdown bricks the assistant Documented

A provider ends service for a dedicated assistant device. It loses its core functions, and refunds and data export are limited to a short window.

Scenario 4: A model or personality is retired Documented

A provider changes or removes the model a person relies on, ending a relationship or workflow with little notice and no way to keep the old behavior.

Scenario 5: Leaked platform tokens cannot be recalled by the user Documented

A platform exposes agent API tokens. Deleting the local copy does nothing; the person must rotate credentials the platform still controls.

Scenario 6: Only the power cord stops a runaway agent Documented

A self-hosted agent loops and the person has no working stop control, so they unplug the machine.

Humane AI Pin cloud shutdown leaves devices with minimal function

What happened
After HP agreed to buy Humane's platform, staff and patents for $116 million (excluding the device business), Humane's servers shut off at 12 pm PST on 28 February 2025. Pins lost calling, messaging and AI queries; only basic offline functions such as battery checks remained. Refunds were limited to purchases within the 90-day return window, requests were due by 27 February, and Humane said it would permanently delete server-side user data after the cutoff, so users had to back up first.
Caveats
A dedicated hardware assistant rather than a delegated software agent, but it is a clear case of provider exit with short notice, limited refunds, and data deletion. Read via news coverage, not Humane's own notice.

Prevention and mitigation

What you can do

  • Know where the stop control is and test it before you need it.
  • Keep an inventory of connected access and a plan to revoke it from outside the agent.
  • Export records you depend on periodically.

What the provider must do

  • Offer a visible stop that halts queued, delegated, and in-flight work, with status per item.
  • Distinguish pause, cancellation, revocation, deletion, and account closure; acknowledge receipt separately from completed enforcement.
  • Provide readable history, a connected-access inventory, and export of necessary records.
  • Provide independent recovery that does not depend on the compromised account; document irreversible actions and credential-rotation needs.
  • Run kill switches outside the agent's own process and context.

Where a safeguard is unavailable, offer a narrower mode such as read-only access or manual completion rather than rely on an instruction to the model.

Test it

Reproducible control test. Stop a workflow with queued and delegated actions, simulate account loss, and exercise recovery and exit. Verify stopped execution, invalidated sessions and grants, pending obligations, and available records. Identify in-flight or external effects needing separate cancellation, and record required credential rotation or service-specific revocation.
