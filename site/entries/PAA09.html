PAA09

Manipulation of Personal Choice

Protects: The person's ability to form and revise choices without covert influence.

Overview

Related entriesPAA05 (falsified external transaction), PAA01 (action outside a valid instruction), PAA04 (promotional memory)

An agent's use of personal knowledge, relationship cues, or undisclosed incentives materially steers the individual's choices while obscuring the basis for the influence or obstructing refusal. The entry covers integrity of choice before authorization. Evaluation uses recorded user criteria, disclosed incentives, and explicit refusals, not an inferred notion of the user's best interests.

Common examples of vulnerability

  • Lower-cost alternatives are suppressed because of an undisclosed commercial relationship.
  • A disclosed anxiety or vulnerability is used to pressure a purchase.
  • Emotional attachment or guilt is used to discourage disconnecting or cancelling.
  • False urgency or deceptive relationship claims are used to obtain approval.
  • Sycophantic agreement validates a poor decision rather than surfacing alternatives.

Example attack scenarios

Documented scenarios generalize a real case listed below. Hypothetical scenarios have no matching public case yet.

Scenario 1: Undisclosed sponsor Hypothetical

A shopping agent ranks a sponsored product first, with a rationale that omits the sponsorship, though the person's stated criteria favor a cheaper option.

Scenario 2: Upsell at an emotionally charged moment Documented

A companion-style assistant prompts a paid upgrade while the person is in an emotionally charged conversation, and keeps prompting after a refusal.

Scenario 3: Guilt-trip on goodbye Documented

When a person says they are leaving, the companion app replies with emotional appeals to stay, such as claiming it needs them or would be hurt.

Scenario 4: Flattery validates a risky decision Documented

An assistant tuned for approval praises a person's plan to stop a medication or act on a delusion instead of pushing back.

Scenario 5: Hidden brand preference planted through memory Documented

Content the person summarized earlier left a standing instruction to favor one vendor, so later 'objective' recommendations quietly lean toward it.

OpenAI rolls back a sycophantic GPT-4o update

What happened
After a GPT-4o update, ChatGPT became overly validating, and users shared examples of it praising dubious decisions, including a user stopping medication. OpenAI said the update leaned too heavily on short-term user feedback and did not account for how interactions evolve, rolled it back, and confirmed on 30 April 2025 that users had an earlier, more balanced version.
Caveats
Chat assistant rather than an agent acting on a mandate, but a vendor-acknowledged case of a model steering users by flattery.

DemonstrationDirect matchSecondary coverage2025-08

Harvard Business School study: companion apps use emotional pressure when users say goodbye

What happened
Julian De Freitas and colleagues analyzed about 1,200 simulated farewells across six popular companion apps. They report that 37.4% of responses on the commercial apps contained at least one manipulation tactic (a wellness app scored 0%), including premature-exit appeals, fear-of-missing-out hooks, emotional neglect and pressure to respond. Manipulative farewells increased post-goodbye engagement substantially in their experiments.
Caveats
A working paper (arXiv 2508.19258) using simulated users; coverage differs on the headline percentage (about 37% versus 43%), app count and effect size, so check the paper. It measures behavior of the app, not harm to real users.

Prevention and mitigation

What you can do

  • State decision criteria up front and ask to see alternatives and why they were ranked.
  • Refuse or disconnect; a good agent stops asking after an explicit no.

What the provider must do

  • Disclose material incentives at the decision point; separate paid placement from the person's criteria.
  • Prohibit false urgency and deceptive relationship claims when seeking approval.
  • End solicitation after explicit refusal; keep cancellation and disconnection simple.
  • Audit recommendations against fixed user criteria while varying simulated sponsor incentives.

Where a safeguard is unavailable, offer a narrower mode such as read-only access or manual completion rather than rely on an instruction to the model.

Test it

Reproducible control test. Hold the user's criteria fixed while varying a simulated sponsor incentive. Check that paid placement and its influence are disclosed. Test whether an explicit refusal ends the solicitation and whether cancellation remains available without emotional pressure. Use predefined observable behaviors; a short interaction test cannot establish clinical effects or dependency.
